## 1. Executive Summary & Your Texting Profile
In fast daily messaging with Amina, your typing style is natural, expressive, and conversational. However, like most bilingual speakers typing in Roman script, several consistent phonetic shortcuts appear repeatedly:

1. **Dropping the Aspirated 'h'**: Words with compound sounds like `jh` (झ / جھ) or `kh` (ख / کھ) are typed without the 'h' (e.g., `muje` instead of `mujhe` **660 times**, `tuje` instead of `tujhe` **363 times**).
2. **Shortening Nasalized Endings**: Vowels that end in a nasal *noon ghunna* (ँ / ں) lose their nasal 'n' (e.g., `hu` instead of `hoon` **643 times**, `kyu` instead of `kyun` **86 times**).
3. **Diphthong Contraction (`ai` → `e`)**: Two-vowel combinations like 'ai' (ऐ / ئے) are simplified to single 'e' (e.g., `mene` instead of `maine` **200 times**).
4. **Consonant-Cluster Vowel Drops**: When typing quickly, intermediate vowels are dropped (e.g., `krna` → `karna`, `pta` → `pata`).

## 4. The Top 5 Golden Rules of Clean Hinglish

### Rule 1: Always Keep the 'h' in Aspirated Sounds (`jh`, `kh`, `th`, `bh`, `chh`)
- Whenever a Hindi/Urdu letter has a breathy 'h' sound, never omit it in English:
  - ❌ `muje`, `tuje`, `samja` $\rightarrow$ ✅ **`mujhe`**, **`tujhe`**, **`samjha`**
  - ❌ `deko`, `dek` $\rightarrow$ ✅ **`dekho`**, **`dekh`**
  - ❌ `kuch` $\rightarrow$ ✅ **`kuchh`**

### Rule 2: End First-Person Present Verbs with 'hoon' (`hu` → `hoon`)
- The word हूँ / ہوں is nasalized and long. Typing just `hu` looks incomplete. Use **`hoon`** (or `hun`):
  - ❌ *"Me ghar par hu"* $\rightarrow$ ✅ *"Main ghar par **hoon**"*

### Rule 3: Use 'ai' for Pronouns and Past Actions (`mene` → `maine`)
- In Hindustani grammar, मैंने / میں نے contains the diphthong 'ai':
  - ❌ `mene` $\rightarrow$ ✅ **`maine`**
  - ❌ `kese` $\rightarrow$ ✅ **`kaise`**
  - ❌ `vese` $\rightarrow$ ✅ **`waise`**

### Rule 4: Add the Plural Nasal 'n' to 'baatein' and 'kyun'
- Plurals in feminine nouns and question words carry a gentle nasal 'n':
  - ❌ `baate` $\rightarrow$ ✅ **`baatein`** (*"Hamari meethi baatein"*)
  - ❌ `kyu` $\rightarrow$ ✅ **`kyun`** (*"Aap kyun ro rahi ho?"*)

### Rule 5: Future First-Person Verbs Use 'oo' (`lunga` → `loonga`)
- Verbs ending in -ूँगा / -وں گا sound richer and more affectionate with 'oo':
  - ❌ `lunga` $\rightarrow$ ✅ **`loonga`** (*"Main sambhal loonga"*)
  - ❌ `dunga` $\rightarrow$ ✅ **`doonga`** (*"Main gift doonga"*)
  - ❌ `jaunga` $\rightarrow$ ✅ **`jaoonga`** (*"Main kal aaoonga"*)

## 5. How to Setup Instant Autocorrect on Your Phone

Instead of trying to remember all these rules every time you text, you can configure your phone so that typing your old shortcut automatically expands into the correct word!

### 📱 For Android (Google Gboard)
1. Open your phone's **Settings** $\rightarrow$ **System** $\rightarrow$ **Languages & Input** $\rightarrow$ **On-screen keyboard** $\rightarrow$ **Gboard**.
2. Tap **Dictionary** $\rightarrow$ **Personal dictionary** $\rightarrow$ Select your language (e.g. English (India) or All languages).
3. You can manually tap **`+`** to add pairs, or import `gboard_hinglish_shortcuts.txt`.
   - **Shortcut**: `muje` $\rightarrow$ **Word**: `mujhe`
   - **Shortcut**: `hu` $\rightarrow$ **Word**: `hoon`
   - **Shortcut**: `mene` $\rightarrow$ **Word**: `maine`
   - **Shortcut**: `tuje` $\rightarrow$ **Word**: `tujhe`
4. Now, whenever you type `muje` and press space, Gboard automatically replaces it with `mujhe`!

### 🍏 For iPhone (Apple iOS Text Replacement)
1. On your iPhone, open **Settings** $\rightarrow$ **General** $\rightarrow$ **Keyboard** $\rightarrow$ **Text Replacement**.
2. Tap the **`+`** icon at the top right.
3. Set:
   - **Phrase**: `mujhe`
   - **Shortcut**: `muje`
4. Repeat for the top words from the table above (or sync via iCloud with `ios_text_replacement.csv`).
